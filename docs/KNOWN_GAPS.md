# Known gaps

Things the portal does today that are weaker than they should be, that were looked at on purpose and not changed yet. Each entry says what is open, what it allows, why it was left, and where it is scheduled. Nothing here is a surprise to the code: where a test pins the current behaviour it is named.

Last reviewed 28 Nov 2026 on branch `phase-4-finance`. Findings marked "verified" were reproduced on the local replay (`scripts/db-replay.sh`, plain PostgreSQL 16 with the Supabase shims, not hosted Supabase).

The gap that stood here before (finance documents and their links written straight to the tables, plan item P0-05 and P1-05) is closed: `register_finance_document` registers a document and `20261128029000_finance_documents_revoke_direct_writes.sql` takes the direct writes away. See `docs/RELEASE_ORDER.md` for the order in which they reach the database.

## 1. One executive can do every step of some money movements alone

Scheduled: not scheduled. The plan names self-review and self-payment of a reimbursement only (decision D4, closed in `20261128028000_finance_self_payment_block.sql`); the rest was looked at while closing that and left as it is. It needs a decision about how many executives the club has and whether a second signature is wanted.

### What is open

Verified on the local replay, as one executive with no second person:

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Void the payment of their own reimbursement (`void_finance_payment`) | allowed: the request goes back to approved and the outflow leaves the ledger. Another executive has to pay it again, but the cash that really left the account is no longer shown as spent in between. |
| Void their own request (`void_finance_expense`) | allowed (the screen hides the button for an own request, the function does not refuse it) |
| Record a club purchase (`record_club_expense`) | allowed, and it is settled at once: there is no approval step and no second person |
| Record income (`record_finance_income`) | allowed |
| Build a funding claim, mark it approved and record the funder's payments (`create_funding_claim`, `set_funding_claim_status`, `record_funding_claim_payment`) | allowed for one executive from start to end; the payment is an inflow with no document behind it |

Every one of them is audited (`finance_audit_log` names the actor), so it is visible afterwards, but nothing stops it.

### Why it is left alone

Which of these steps should need a second person is a decision for the club, and the plan has not made it. Self-payment was chosen because it is the one where the person who gains is the person who acts.

## 2. The membership gate: what it does not do (P0-01, Phase 4.1)

Added with `20261128010000_membership_gate_schema.sql` and `20261128010100_membership_gate_signup_default.sql` (order of release in `docs/RELEASE_ORDER.md`). A new sign-up is `pending` until an active executive or administrator approves it in the Sign-up requests queue; a declined one is `rejected` and stays out until an executive approves it. Tests: `supabase/tests/76_membership_gate*.test.sql`, `supabase/tests/concurrency/76_membership_gate_*.sh`, `tests/component/signup-requests.test.tsx`, `tests/component/shell-membership-gate.test.tsx`. What the gate leaves open on purpose:

- **Anyone with a Google account can create a pending profile.** Decision D1 is "needs an executive's approval", not "restricted to a domain". Each stranger costs one `profiles` and one `auth.users` row and an entry in the queue (the queue lists the 500 oldest waiting; the count in the menu is exact). There is no rate limit and no clean-up of old pending accounts. Fix when it matters: an allowed-domain list checked in `private.handle_new_user`, or a scheduled delete of sign-ups pending for more than N days (`admin_delete_member` already removes one).
- **The first administrator's address is written in `private.handle_new_user` too** (as an exemption from `pending`), next to the place `20260812000000_admin_console.sql` promotes it. Both go with the bootstrap handover of Phase 4.4 (P3-04).
- **Nobody is told.** An executive learns of a sign-up from the count in the menu only (no email); the person learns of an approval by pressing Check again or signing in again. A declined person is not told why: the reason is for executives and is never in any answer a pending or rejected account can read.
- **The queue shows the 50 most recently declined.** An older declined sign-up stays rejected and cannot be approved from the screen (the function still takes it: `review_pending_member(id, true)`), and the person cannot sign up again with the same Google account. An administrator can delete the account so that they start over.
- **The sign-in form no longer asks for a name (Phase 3, P3-06).** After the first sign-in an approved member sees a "Complete your profile" step once (name, phone, class year, specialty; "Skip for now"). There is no column that says the step was done, so "not completed" means no phone, class year or specialty plus a per-browser flag for "skipped" (without storage the step is offered at every visit to the overview). A `profiles.profile_completed_at` column, returned by `get_portal_bootstrap`, would make it exact: a decision for the owner.
- **The finance functions say "Your access to the portal has been deactivated" to a pending or rejected caller.** They had their own explicit `is_active_member` check before this change. Every table write and every other function says the right thing ("waiting for approval", "not approved"). Cosmetic: the account is refused either way.
- **Approve has no confirmation.** It is one click and an executive can undo a mistake only by deactivating the account (an administrator). Declining is the one that asks (for a reason) and is undone by Approve.

## 3. What the security headers do not cover (P3-02, Phase 4.4)

Added in `next.config.ts` (a Content-Security-Policy and five other headers on every response; written up at the top of that file). Reviewed 28 Nov 2026 on branch `phase-4-platform`, verified in a real browser against the built site. Tests: `tests/security-headers.test.mjs` (the header list, 28 mutations of it caught) and `tests/browser/specs/csp.spec.mjs` (the policy against every screen, the recorder, the player, signed files in `<img>`, `<audio>`, `<video>`, `<iframe>` and new tabs, and what it must refuse); the browser suite's guard fails any test on a policy violation, a missing header or a request to an origin that is not the app, its Supabase project and the one named hero picture. What the headers leave open:

- **Inline scripts are allowed (`script-src 'unsafe-inline'`), so the policy is not a defence against script injection.** The landing page is prerendered and Next writes its hydration data into inline `<script>` tags whose text changes with every build and every public concert; a hash cannot be put in a header ahead of time, and a nonce needs one render per request, which would turn the cached landing page into a dynamic one. What stands in the way today is the code, not the header: the source has no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, `innerHTML` or `document.write` (searched 28 Nov 2026), React escapes every text, and the address of a library link must start with `http://` or `https://` in the database (the `resources_node_shape` check of `20260907000000_unified_resources_library.sql`), so a `javascript:` address cannot be stored. `unsafe-eval` is not allowed in production (a test pins it; `next dev` adds it). Fix when it matters: a per-request nonce from `proxy.ts` (costs the five-minute static landing page) or a hash list if the framework offers one, and the unit test's list of allowed sources changes with it.
- **Styles are allowed inline (`style-src 'unsafe-inline'`)**: wavesurfer.js writes a `<style>` element into the player it draws. Style injection can leak far less than script injection, and is the same answer: there is no way to inject markup today.
- **One picture still comes from another website.** The photograph beside the headline of the landing and sign-in screens loads from `asianartsagency.co.uk`, by its exact address in `img-src`; every view tells that site the visitor's IP address and browser, and nobody holds a licence for it. Needs a person: `docs/ASSETS.md` ("needs: `Trio-WP.jpg`") says what to do and which line of `next.config.ts` to empty when it is self-hosted (a test fails until it is).
- **The whole Supabase project is trusted for pictures, media, frames and connections**, not only the club's buckets. A file a member may upload to a bucket they can write to can be shown in a frame of the portal, with the type it was uploaded under. It runs in Supabase's origin, so it cannot read the portal's session, but the frame is not sandboxed (the `sandbox` attribute stops Chrome's built-in PDF viewer from working). The control against an uploaded web page is the list of allowed file types in `20261128030600_storage_bucket_limits.sql` (no HTML, no SVG), which is released after the frontend and is **not applied until then** (`docs/RELEASE_ORDER.md`, section 3).
- **A file opened in its own tab carries none of our headers.** Supabase serves signed files itself: checked on the test project, they come with no Content-Security-Policy and no `X-Frame-Options`, and with `access-control-allow-origin: *`. The portal opens them with `noopener,noreferrer`, so the tab has no link back (tested), and that is all that protects the portal from what the file does there.
- **Pages that are not ours have no headers from us.** The platform's own error pages (a deployment that does not exist, an outage of the host) and every answer of Supabase come from somebody else.
- **Nobody is told when a policy blocks something for a real member.** There is no `report-to` and no reporting address; a refusal shows only in that person's browser console. If Supabase serves files from a different host one day, previews would stop working for everyone and the first sign would be a member saying so. The suites catch a change in this repository, not a change in the platform. `docs/ops-templates/error-reporting-options.md` lists what could be done and what it costs.
- **The policy is fixed when the site is built.** It names the Supabase project from `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` at `next build`. A build made with a wrong or missing address cannot reach its backend at all (it fails closed: no host is allowed and `frame-src` is `'none'`), and changing the variable in Vercel does nothing until the next deployment. A Preview deployment uses whatever its own variables say (`docs/OWNER_CHECKLIST.md`, item 12).
- **The camera is allowed for the portal.** `Permissions-Policy` switches off everything a page can ask a device for (location, payment, USB, serial, HID, MIDI, screen capture, motion sensors) except `microphone=(self)` and `camera=(self)`; the recording studio has a video mode (`use-recording-media.ts`). Remove the video mode and the camera entry goes with it.
- **No cross-origin isolation and no HSTS from the repository.** `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` is `same-origin-allow-popups` (Google sign-in is a full-page redirect, not a popup; this value would still let a popup talk back). There is no `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy` and no `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy`: nothing the portal does needs the page to be isolated, and other sites may embed its logo. `Strict-Transport-Security` is left to Vercel, which sends it on every HTTPS response; a different host would lose it. `docs/OWNER_CHECKLIST.md` has the one-line check after the first real deployment.
- **A probe of `eval` has to run from the page.** The browser suite proves that `script-src` refuses `eval`, but code run through the browser's debugging protocol (what Playwright's `page.evaluate` uses) is exempt, so the probe is a timer written by an inline script. A test written with `page.evaluate` alone would pass on a policy that allows `unsafe-eval`; the comment in the spec says so.

## 4. Lesser notes from the second security review (28 Nov 2026), not fixed

The independent review of Phase 4 found three defects that were fixed (`20261128032000`, `20261128032100`, `20261128032200`, written up in `REMEDIATION_STATUS.md`, "Found by the second review"). These are the smaller things it noted and that were left on purpose.

- **`resources.metadata` (jsonb) has no size limit.** Any manager can store a very large object in it. The portal never selects the column (`RESOURCE_FIELDS` in `lib/api/resources.ts` is "every column but `metadata`"), so nobody downloads it; the cost is table bloat only. Not fixed: a limit needs a decision on what the column is for (nothing writes it today except the legacy copy).
- **The audit trail leaves some changes out, by design.** Archiving and unarchiving a resource or a group, and publishing an announcement, are not logged as separate actions; rows created by an import (`import_id` set) are not logged one by one, only the import. `content_audit_log` is a trail of who changed what, not a complete history of every status flip. The list of what is logged is the trigger list of `20261128030500_content_audit_log.sql` and `supabase/tests/77_content_audit_log.test.sql`.
- **A pending or declined account that calls a finance function is told "Your access to the portal has been deactivated".** Wrong wording, harmless: the call is refused. It is the same gap as in section 2 above (the finance functions test `is_active_member` themselves); every other function and table says "waiting for approval".
- **The actor guard (`20261128030200`) does not fire for a write made by another trigger or a foreign-key action (`pg_trigger_depth() > 1`).** It is reachable only with arbitrary SQL, which neither PostgREST nor Storage lets a signed-in person run; the definer functions that write who-columns set them from `auth.uid()` themselves.
- **An amount of `Infinity` is refused by the column, with the database's own words.** `numeric(12,2)` answers 22003 "numeric field overflow" before a row exists, so nothing is stored; the sentence for zero and negative amounts ("amount must be greater than zero") is only used for NaN, zero, negative and a sum that rounds to 0.00. Nobody can type Infinity in a number box.
- **`recordings.duration_seconds` is an unbounded `numeric`** and accepts NaN or Infinity; the recording screens only display it. It is not money and is listed as an exception in `supabase/tests/78_nan_amounts.test.sql`.
- **The folder rules of `20261128032200` do not apply to executives,** and the new triggers do not clean rows that exist already (a row planted before them stays until somebody deletes it: the query is in `RELEASE_ORDER.md`, "Manual steps", item 5). An executive can already read, overwrite and delete every library, archive and finance file, so a row of theirs adds nothing.
